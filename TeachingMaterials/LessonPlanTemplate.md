🟠 **Figure Activity: Why should you care about the quality of figures?**

1. **Introduce the activity:** We will show some figures and highlight the main point of each.
2. Show **Figure 1** with no context, and give 30 seconds for people to analyze and assess.
3. 🔵 Slido poll questions: _"Who would you be comfortable sharing this with?"_ and _"What is the main point of this figure?"_ ([Access link][slido])
4. **Fixing the basics:** Highlight what is wrong with it. Use it as a teaching example, referring to poll responses. What could use attention?
5. Introduce **Figure 2** in contrast to Figure 1.
   - 🟠 **Open discussion:** What changed between Figure 1 and Figure 2? What is still missing? (Slide 14)
   - **Lesson:** The data format has not changed, but the content has (format vs. content). Discuss how different formats can be used to highlight content and build understanding.
6. Introduce **Figure 3** versus Figure 2.
   - 🟠 **Open discussion:** What changed between Figure 2 and Figure 3? What is still missing? (Slide 16)
   - Reflect with the group on the changes in content from Figure 2 to Figure 3: a similar graph type, but with the content shifted to be more presentable.
7. Introduce **Figure 4**.
   - 🟠 **Open discussion:** What changed between Figure 3 and Figure 4? What is still missing?
   - **Lesson:** Content can be specific to the audience; the needs of a figure change over time.
8. Highlight the 🔴 **Figure Spectrum handout** and how the figure changes through iterations over time, across different contexts, and through feedback from advisors, mentors, and collaborators.

### Figure Rubric Categories

Introduce the Figure Rubric categories with examples:

- **Low-hanging fruit of figure creation:**
  - Scale & Resolution
  - Units & Labels
- **Higher-level figure categories:**
  - Colors
  - Emphasis
  - Ink:Content Ratio
- **The highest, most complex goal:**
  - Accessibility

### Posters

Discuss the important features of creating posters:

- A poster should stand on its own
- Size of fonts/text matters
- Consistent poster color palette
- A poster interfaces with a person standing alongside it
- Less is more
- Recap of the poster's main talking points

### Presentations
**5 minutes · 10:55 – 11:00**

Discuss the important parts of presentation figures:

- Start the activity with a poor presentation (presenting all the content at once) versus a good presentation (presenting it in pieces)
- Use animations and transparency; you do not need to show ALL of your paper figures
- Show a subset of the data
- Table vs. figure for presentation formats
- Recap of the presentation's main talking points

### 🟠 Discussion

- 🟠 What are the primary differences you see between posters and presentations?

### Publications
**5 minutes · 11:02 – 11:07**

Discuss the important features of publications:

- Papers are academic currency: putting your best figure forward is important
- **The first figure is the screenshot figure!** This is the figure many others will screenshot for their own Related Work slides.
- Use a multi-panel display to create a nice figure
- Use schematics alongside data for ease of understanding
- Pick your paper palette and keep colors consistent throughout
- Recap of the publication's main talking points

### Recap

- **One size does not fit all:** Each figure can be customized into different formats. Following the Figure Rubric creates figures that are lossless and saves time when adjusting for each format.
